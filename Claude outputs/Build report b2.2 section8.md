5. **No images yet.** `mcq_image` and `listen_choose_image` are unused for that
   reason; at A1 they matter, and image sourcing is the next media task.
6. **B2 is complete.** Section 8 added 36 grammar points (G-243…G-278),
   9 phonology points (P-070…P-078), 18 functions (F-113…F-130), 648 lexemes,
   18 story episodes and 54 comprehension questions. Sections 7 and 8 together
   are the whole of B2, and their syllabus — like B1's — was written from scratch.
7. **The per-section lexeme target was derived three times and came out
   differently each time.** Section 6 shipped 626 against a nominal 800 because
   B1 vocabulary was spent; Section 7 reached 912 at 50 per unit because B2
   registers reopen the lexicon; Section 8 measured a free pool of 665, cut 73
   words that belong to one discipline's own literature rather than to B2.2, and
   shipped 648 at 36 per unit. The rule that keeps being right: derive the target
   from what is actually left, never copy a number down a level. The 73 cut words
   (`antinomy`, `palimpsest`, `habitus`, `anaphora`, `chiaroscuro`, …) are real
   English and belong at C1, not padding at B2.2.
8. **Both B2 sections satisfy docs/00 §4.1 rule 7.** Every B2.1 and B2.2 lexeme
   carries an English definition as well as an L1 gloss. Gate G25 blocks a
   B2-or-later record without one and reports the 1,425-record B1 backlog rather
   than pretending it complies.
9. **A third wave of missing everyday words — and this time the pattern is
   sharper.** Section 6 exposed 22, Section 7 exposed 60, Section 8 exposed 32,
   and the 32 fall into three named shapes rather than a list of accidents.
   *Half a set was taught and half was not*: `north` and `south` were in the
   course, `east` and `west` were not. *A word was taught in one part of speech
   only*: `hard` and `early` existed as adverbs but not as adjectives, so
   `harder` and `earlier` were not English the course knew; `judge` was a noun
   and never a verb. *A derived form was assumed because its base existed*:
   `write` gave no `writer`, `learn` no `learner`, `simple` no `simply`.
   Separately, the course teaches `sentence`, `word`, `clause`, `phrase`,
   `paragraph` and `comma` but had never taught `noun`, `verb`, `adjective` or
   `adverb` — the metalanguage a grammar course uses on every page. All 32 are
   now taught where a learner first needs them, not where Section 8 tripped over
   them (`content/lexemes/src-gap-topup-3.json`).
10. **A fourth builder defect of the same class: `die` produced `diing`.** A verb
   in -ie swaps to -y before -ing (dying, lying, tying). This is the fourth
   generator bug that invented English — after `agreeed`, `carefuler` and
   `parentses` — and the class matters more than any instance: an invented form
   still enters `available_forms`, so the vocabulary gate would have *accepted*
   text using it. That is how `owner` stayed untaught for five sections.
11. **Fifteen of the 54 Section 8 comprehension questions were answerable without
   reading the episode**, because the keyed answer was the longest option. No
   gate blocks this — G07 only warns — but a question a learner can win on shape
   alone is not a comprehension question. All fifteen had their distractors
   rewritten to full length, each still wrong for a reason a reader of the
   episode could name.
12. **Ten Section 8 IPA transcriptions were wrong and no gate could see it.**
   `demarcate` had first-syllable stress, `covert` had the British pattern,
   `transgress` had /z/ for /s/, `heretofore` had /tuː/ for a schwa, and six
   others. A wrong IPA string is schema-valid, and it is what the audio and every
   pronunciation item are generated from, so it teaches a wrong word silently.
   Read every transcription aloud before shipping a section.
13. **Fourteen glosses were word-for-word identical to another lexeme's** —
   `franchise` and `suffrage` shared one Uzbek and one Russian gloss, as did
   `mutual` and `reciprocal`. That makes a pair indistinguishable in the
   learner's own language, which is worst precisely where the unit teaches the
   pair to be told apart. Each is now narrowed to the sense its English
   definition states.
14. **193 band-1000 lexemes are still first taught at B1 or later** — gate G24,
   unchanged since Section 6. Each section's top-up clears the words its own
   stories need; the backlog itself is a Sections 1-4 job and should be done as
   one pass, not eight.
15. **Section 9 (C1, units 121-144) is next** — 24 units, the largest section so
   far, and the first where the C1 specialist layer deliberately left aside at
   B2.2 comes into play. The pipeline needs no changes; the C1 syllabus has to be
   authored from scratch.

## 5. How to rebuild

```
./build.sh
```

which runs, in order:

```
python3 tools/build_lexemes.py   # compact sources  -> build/lexemes.json
python3 tools/build_units.py     # unit specs       -> build/units/*.json
python3 tools/build_items.py     # everything above -> build/items/*.json
python3 tools/validate.py        # the CI gates (exit 1 blocks a merge)
python3 tools/report.py          # this file
```

Builds are deterministic (`random.seed(20260908)`), so the same sources always
produce the same item ids.
