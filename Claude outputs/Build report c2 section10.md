## 4. Known gaps and the authoring TODO

The course is complete: 168 units, A1 to C2. What follows is what is still open
and what the ten sections taught, in that order.

### Still open

1. **All audio is referenced but not recorded.** Human voices are required below
   C1 (docs/15 §5). Every `audio` path in the build points at a file that does
   not exist yet; this is the single largest remaining task.
2. **No images yet.** `mcq_image` and `listen_choose_image` are unused for that
   reason; at A1 they matter, and image sourcing is the next media task.
3. **Item volume runs at roughly 60-70% of the modelled lesson time.** Each
   node-tier averages ~13 items where ~16-18 are needed. The remainder should be
   hand-authored variety, not more of the same templates.
4. **Reading sits well under its 5% target in the early sections.** Honest
   rather than fixable by generating more items: at A1 there is very little text
   long enough to read. The fix is graded readers in the Immersion Engine
   (docs/06 §7).
5. **Grammar items run a few points below target.** Each grammar point has
   exactly one home node, which is correct; close the shortfall with
   hand-authored contrast items, not more generated variants.
6. **193 band-1000 lexemes are still first taught at B1 or later** (gate G24,
   warning). Unchanged since Section 6 — five sections have now cleared the words
   their own stories needed without touching the backlog. It wants one pass over
   Sections 1-4, not a sixth round of fragments.
7. **1,425 B1 lexemes still carry only an L1 gloss** where docs/00 §4.1 rule 7
   asks for an L2 definition too (gate G25, warning). Every lexeme from B2 up has
   one; the backlog is entirely below that line.
8. **IPA notation is inconsistent across the corpus.** 1,693 of 5,948
   transcriptions write the long vowels with a length mark (/uː/, /iː/) and the
   rest write them bare. Both are valid General American notation, but the audio
   pipeline will read these strings, and Sections 1-7 hold almost all of the
   marked ones. Normalising is a mechanical pass; it is listed here because
   nothing currently fails on it.
9. **694 generated MCQ items have very uneven option lengths** (gate G07,
   warning). See finding 14 below: this is the machine-generated tail of a defect
   that was also the most persistent hand-authored one.

### What the ten sections taught

10. **C2 is authored, and it is the largest section.** Section 10 added 48 grammar
   points (G-327…G-374), 12 phonology points (P-091…P-102), 24 functions
   (F-155…F-178), 543 lexemes, 24 story episodes and 72 comprehension questions.
   Its grammar is almost entirely about choosing rather than forming: contested
   usage, irony, litotes, implicature, register shift, nominalisation as
   concealment, false balance, legal drafting against plain English, proportional
   summary, editing another person's prose, and knowing when a text is finished.
11. **Every section's honest vocabulary target came out different from the flat
   number in the spec, five times running.** Section 6 shipped 626 against a
   nominal 800. Section 7 reached 912 at 50 per unit. Section 8 measured 665, cut
   73 and shipped 648. Section 9 probed 409 "C1" candidates and found **195
   already taught**, shipping 421 at ~18 per unit and carrying its weight in
   grammar instead. Section 10 measured 631 and cut 88: **26 as archaic** (words
   no longer live in modern English) and **62 as one-discipline** (words that live
   inside a single literature), leaving **543** at ~23 per unit. The C2 cut used a
   test none of the earlier ones could: there is no level above to defer a word
   to, so the only question left is whether the word is still in use. Measuring
   first was right every time.
12. **A fifth wave of missing everyday words — 32 of them — and the same three
   shapes.** *A derived form assumed*: `help` without `helpful` or `unhelpful`,
   `difficult` without `difficulty`, `long` without `length`, `honest` without
   `dishonest`, `beautiful` without `beauty`, `breathe` without `breath`.
   *One part of speech only*: `double`, `hang`, `blow`, `let`, `attack` and `shot`
   existed as one part of speech and never the other. *Never taught at all*:
   `beside`, **`poor`**, `ugly`, `piece`, `space`, `dot`, `dull`, `remove`,
   `underneath`, `obey`, `entire`, `proper`, `stumble`, `current`, `consistent`.
   That `poor` reached unit 168 untaught is the sharpest single illustration of
   why the vocabulary gate exists. Running total across Sections 6-10: **201
   everyday words** the course had never taught, every one of them found by a gate
   and not by a reader.
13. **Reading every transcription before shipping worked.** Sections 8 and 9 shipped
   with 10 and 8 wrong IPA transcriptions, caught only by hand afterwards. Section
   10 was read as it was written and the audit found **5** errors in 575 records
   (`lambast` with the FACE vowel, `catholic` missing its medial vowel, `prolix`,
   `nadir` and `renege`) plus 15 notation departures. The defect did not go away;
   reading at authoring time moved it from ~2% to ~0.9%. There is still no gate
   that can see it, because the schema will accept any string.
14. **The question-length defect was fixed by construction, not by inspection.**
   Fifteen of 54 questions in Section 8 and sixteen of 72 in Section 9 were
   answerable on option length alone: the keyed answer ends up longest because it
   is the one written carefully. Section 10's 72 questions were built with a
   four-option length spread capped at 14 characters and checked mechanically
   before the build — **0 out of balance**. The same check applied to the
   generated items is gate G07, which still reports 694.
15. **Four interference links were miscategorised, and G14 caught one of them.**
   `pellucid`→`opaque`, `abstruse`→`pellucid`, `insipid`→`piquant` and
   `dissonance`→`euphony` were tagged as interference — the flag that keeps two
   words out of one node. They are antonyms, which are taught together on purpose.
   The gate failed on the pair that happened to land in one node; the other three
   were passing by luck. Fixing the flags was the repair, not moving the words.
16. **Two teaching claims in the C2 stories were wrong and had to be rewritten.**
   An episode asserted that "no good writer has ever obeyed" the split-infinitive
   rule (false — many careful writers avoid the split deliberately), and another
   stated that litotes always lands between the two poles (false — "not bad" can
   mean very good). Both were repaired to claims that are true. Nothing in the
   build could have found either: the vocabulary gate reads these episodes as word
   lists, not as assertions about English.
17. **Two defect classes remain outside every gate.** Wrong IPA and false
   statements about English in the story text are both invisible to the pipeline,
   and both were found only by reading. Any future authoring of this content
   should budget a read-through, because the gates will report a clean build over
   either one.

## 5. How to rebuild

```
./build.sh
```

which runs, in order:

```
python3 tools/build_lexemes.py   # compact sources  -> build/lexemes.json
python3 tools/build_units.py     # unit specs       -> build/units/*.json
python3 tools/build_items.py     # everything above -> build/items/*.json
python3 tools/validate.py        # the CI gates (exit 1 blocks a merge)
python3 tools/report.py          # this file
```

Builds are deterministic (`random.seed(20260908)`), so the same sources always
produce the same item ids.
