## 2. LISTENING

### 2.1 Sub-skills, in teaching order

| # | Sub-skill | Description | Level |
|---|-----------|-------------|-------|
| 1 | Phonemic discrimination | Distinguishing minimal pairs in stream | A1 |
| 2 | Word-boundary segmentation | Finding where words start and end — *a nice cold beer* vs *an ice-cold beer* | A1→B1 |
| 3 | Stress and rhythm perception | Hearing which syllables are prominent | A1→A2 |
| 4 | Gist / global comprehension | Getting the main idea | A1 |
| 5 | Listening for specific information (scanning) | Numbers, names, times | A1 |
| 6 | Listening for detail | Full propositional content | A2→B1 |
| 7 | Decoding weak forms and contractions | *can/can't*, *should've* | A2→B1 |
| 8 | Decoding connected speech | Linking, elision, assimilation | B1 |
| 9 | Following discourse structure | Signposts, sequence, digression | B1→B2 |
| 10 | Inferring the unstated | Implication, presupposition | B2 |
| 11 | Identifying speaker attitude & stance | Prosody + lexis | B2 |
| 12 | Distinguishing fact from opinion | | B2 |
| 13 | Handling overlap and interruption | Multi-party talk | B2→C1 |
| 14 | Coping with degraded/accented input | | B2→C1 |
| 15 | Recognising irony, humour, sarcasm | | C1 |
| 16 | Note-taking while listening | Simultaneous processing | B2→C1 |
| 17 | Listening critically (bias, rhetoric) | | C1→C2 |
| 18 | Real-time processing at native speed with no repetition | | C2 |

### 2.2 The two modes — both are required

- **Intensive listening** (bottom-up, in-app): short items, repeated, transcript-supported, targeted at specific decoding problems. Includes **dictation**, the single most diagnostic listening exercise ever invented. Dictation reveals exactly which sounds and forms the learner cannot decode.
- **Extensive listening** (top-down, Immersion Engine): long, enjoyable, comprehensible input at high volume, without testing. Podcasts, audiobooks, video. **Volume matters more than difficulty.**

### 2.3 Strategy training (explicit)
Predicting from context/title/images · activating schema before listening · tolerating ambiguity (the single most important beginner skill: *keep listening past the word you missed*) · using redundancy · attending to stressed words first · monitoring comprehension · asking for repetition/clarification · using visual cues · post-listening self-check against transcript.

### 2.4 The "listening cliff" protocol (B1.1 — a signature feature)
Learners who understand the app's audio but not real English are hitting connected speech, not vocabulary. The protocol:
1. Listen to a natural-speed authentic clip. Fail.
2. Listen again with the **decoded transcript** showing the actual phonetic realisation: *"whaddaya wanna do"* under *"What do you want to do?"*
3. Shadow the natural version 5 times.
4. Listen again, unsupported. Succeed.
5. Repeat with 8 clips per week for 6 weeks.
This single protocol closes the most common intermediate plateau.

## 3. READING

### 3.1 Sub-skills

decoding & sight-word automaticity (A1) · chunking (reading in phrases, not words) (A1→A2) · skimming for gist (A2) · scanning for specific information (A2) · intensive reading for detail (A2→B1) · inferring word meaning from context (B1) · recognising text organisation (B1) · distinguishing main idea from supporting detail (B1) · following an argument (B2) · inferring implicit meaning (B2) · identifying author stance, tone and purpose (B2) · evaluating evidence and detecting bias (C1) · appreciating style and register (C1) · reading across texts and synthesising (C1) · critical and rhetorical reading (C2) · reading literature for aesthetic effect (C2).

### 3.2 Word-attack strategy (taught B1)
Order of attack for an unknown word: (1) is it needed for the task? If not, skip; (2) part of speech from position; (3) affixes and root; (4) local context; (5) wider context; (6) cognate check (with a false-friend warning); (7) dictionary. **Teaching learners to skip is as important as teaching them to look up.**

### 3.3 Extensive reading programme (mandatory from A2)

| Level | Reader band | Headwords | Books/period | Words/period |
|-------|-------------|----------:|-------------:|-------------:|
| A1.2 | Starter | 300 | 4 short | 5,000 |
| A2.1 | Beginner | 600 | 4 | 12,000 |
| A2.2 | Elementary | 1,000 | 4 | 18,000 |
| B1.1 | Pre-intermediate | 1,400 | 5 | 40,000 |
| B1.2 | Intermediate | 1,800 | 5 | 60,000 |
| B2.1 | Upper-intermediate | 2,500 | 5 | 90,000 |
| B2.2 | Advanced graded | 3,000 | 5 | 120,000 |
| C1 | Unsimplified contemporary | — | 6+ | 600,000 |
| C2 | Any, incl. pre-1950 & literary | — | 15+ | 1,300,000 |

**Rules of extensive reading (Day & Bamford, adapted):** learner chooses the book · it must be easy (≤ 2 unknown words per 100) · reading is for pleasure, not testing · dictionary use is discouraged during reading · faster is better · the teacher/app reads too (recommendation feed) · a book that isn't working is abandoned, not endured.

**In-app support:** tap-to-gloss with the learner's own known-word list applied (glossing only what they don't know) · one-tap "add to my SRS" · reading-speed tracker · a "% of this book you already know" pre-check that recommends level-appropriate titles.

## 4. SPEAKING

### 4.1 Sub-skills

articulating phonemes intelligibly · word stress and rhythm · linking and fluent connected speech · intonation for meaning · chunking speech into tone units · fluency (speed, pausing, hesitation control) · accuracy under time pressure · lexical retrieval speed · turn-taking · repair and reformulation · circumlocution (talking around an unknown word) · discourse structuring in monologue · register control · interactive listening (responding to what was actually said) · negotiating meaning · managing communication breakdown.

### 4.2 The fluency-building toolkit (all implemented as exercise types)

| Technique | What it does | Level |
|-----------|--------------|-------|
| **Repeat-after** | Articulatory accuracy | A1 |
| **Read-aloud** | Grapheme-phoneme + prosody | A1→B1 |
| **Shadowing** (simultaneous) | Prosody, rhythm, speed — the highest-return speaking technique for accent and fluency | A2→C2 |
| **Chorusing / backchaining** | Long phrase fluency (*…in the morning / go to work in the morning / I go to work in the morning*) | A1→B1 |
| **4/3/2 technique** | Same 4-min talk delivered in 4, then 3, then 2 minutes to a new listener. Forces automaticity. **The single best-evidenced fluency drill.** | B1→C1 |
| **Substitution drills** | Pattern automaticity | A1→A2 |
| **Information gap** | Meaning negotiation | A2→B2 |
| **Picture description / spot-the-difference** | Vocabulary retrieval under pressure | A1→B1 |
| **Role-play with AI partner** | Interaction in a safe setting | A1→C2 |
| **Debate / discussion** | Argument under pressure | B2→C2 |
| **Impromptu speech (60-second challenge)** | Speaking without planning | B1→C2 |
| **Retell** | Reconstructing content in own words | A2→C2 |
| **Think-aloud narration** | Producing continuously | B1→C2 |
| **Self-recording + self-transcription** | Noticing one's own errors — the strongest single accuracy intervention | B1→C2 |

### 4.3 The accuracy/fluency alternation rule
Fluency tasks and accuracy tasks must never be merged. A speaking node is explicitly labelled:
- **Fluency mode:** no interruption, no error highlighting during the task, feedback only on content and communication success. Errors logged silently.
- **Accuracy mode:** immediate correction, form focus, repetition until correct.
The learner sees which mode they are in. This removes the anxiety that stops learners speaking at all.

### 4.4 Circumlocution training (B1 — a differentiator)
Explicitly teach the strategies for when the word is missing: superordinate (*a kind of tool*) · function (*it's for cutting paper*) · appearance (*it's small and metal*) · analogy (*it's like a knife but…*) · example (*like when you…*) · approximation · asking (*What do you call the thing that…?*). Drill with a game where the target word is **banned**. This converts a frozen learner into a communicating one faster than any amount of vocabulary.

### 4.5 Anxiety management (design requirement)
Speaking anxiety is the #1 cause of abandonment in speaking features. Mitigations that must ship: private-by-default recordings · unlimited retakes with only the best kept · no live scoring bar during recording · text-input fallback for every speaking task · "whisper mode" using near-field ASR · optional avatar instead of camera · warm, non-judgemental feedback copy ("Almost — the *i* in *ship* is shorter" not "Incorrect").

## 5. WRITING

### 5.1 Sub-skills

handwriting/typing the Latin alphabet · spelling · punctuation · sentence construction · sentence variety · paragraph construction (topic sentence + support + link) · cohesion · genre conventions · register control · planning and outlining · drafting · revising for content · editing for accuracy · proofreading · citing sources · adapting to word limits · writing for a specified reader.

### 5.2 Process writing (from B1, mandatory)
Every extended writing task follows: **brainstorm → outline → draft → feedback → revise → edit → publish**. The app must support multi-stage submission; a single-shot "write and get a score" flow teaches nothing about writing.

### 5.3 Feedback model

| Stage | Feedback type | Who/what |
|-------|---------------|----------|
| Draft 1 | **Content and organisation only** — no grammar correction | LLM rater with rubric + optional peer |
| Draft 2 | **Indirect correction** — errors underlined with a code (`WW` wrong word, `WF` word form, `T` tense, `Prep`, `Art`, `SV` agreement, `WO` word order, `Sp`, `P`, `Frag`, `RO`) but **not** corrected. The learner self-corrects. | Automatic |
| Draft 3 | **Direct correction** of what remains + a reformulation of 2 sentences showing a more native-like version | Automatic + human audit sample |
| After | 2 error types added to the learner's personal error log and scheduled | Scheduler |

Research consistently shows indirect coded feedback outperforms direct correction for long-term accuracy. Direct correction is used only after the learner has had a chance to self-repair.

### 5.4 Writing genres by level
See doc 05 §6. Each genre ships with: a model text, an annotated model (moves highlighted), a checklist, a phrase bank, and a rubric.

### 5.5 Anti-plagiarism and AI-use policy
From B2, tasks that could be completed by an AI assistant are complemented by in-app, timed, offline-detectable production. The product's position is stated to learners plainly: using AI to write your practice text is like using a forklift at the gym. Detection is not the mechanism; task design is — timed, personalised, spoken-follow-up-on-your-own-text tasks.

## 7. THE IMMERSION ENGINE

### 7.1 Components

| Component | Description | From |
|-----------|-------------|------|
| **Graded Library** | 400+ readers across 8 bands with audio, tap-gloss, and known-word coverage estimates | A1.2 |
| **Story Mode** | Original serialised fiction written to the syllabus — the same characters across all 10 sections, so learners return for the story, not the streak | A1.1 |
| **Podcast Feed** | Level-tagged episodes with interactive transcripts, dual-speed audio, and clip-to-SRS | A2.1 |
| **Video Lab** | Short authentic video with three subtitle modes (off / English / English+gloss), clip looping, and shadowing recorder | A2.2 |
| **News Digest** | The same news story rewritten at A2/B1/B2/C1 and in the original — learners climb the same content | B1.1 |
| **Song Studio** | Lyric gap-fill and connected-speech decoding | A2.1 |
| **AI Conversation Partner** | Roleplay with configurable persona, speed, accent, patience, and correction level | A1.2 |
| **Writing Studio** | Multi-draft writing with process feedback | B1.1 |
| **Live Human Practice** | Scheduled conversation with peers or tutors, with a structured task and a post-session error report | B1.2 |
| **Book Club** | Cohort reads one book per month with discussion prompts | B2.1 |
| **Field Kit** | Vocabulary harvesting from the learner's own material (paste an article, upload a PDF, share from any app) → auto-glossed, auto-scheduled | B1.2 |

### 7.2 The immersion contract
From B1, the path is **gated on immersion volume**, not only on lessons. A learner cannot unlock Section 7 without logging the B1.2 reading and listening minimums. This is unusual and will be unpopular with a minority of users; it is also the only way the C2 promise is not a lie. Softening: the gate is generous, the tracking is automatic, and any external reading/listening can be self-declared with spot-check comprehension questions.

### 7.3 Comprehensible-input compliance
Every item in the Immersion Engine carries a computed **known-word coverage** for the individual learner (their SRS-known set ÷ the text's tokens). The feed sorts by coverage:
- **95–98% coverage** → recommended for extensive (pleasure) input
- **90–95%** → recommended for intensive (study) input
- **< 90%** → shown with a warning; available but flagged as frustrating
- **> 98%** → flagged as "easy — great for speed and fluency"

This single number, computed per learner per text, is the core intelligence of the Immersion Engine.
