# 01 — CEFR LEVEL ARCHITECTURE
## Sub-levels, can-do descriptors, qualitative parameters, exit gates

This document defines **what each level means operationally**. Every syllabus item in docs 02–06 is tagged to one of the sub-levels defined here, and every exit gate in doc 12 tests against the criteria defined here.

---

## 1. The level ladder

| Section | Sub-level | Common name | Cumulative hours | Certificate wording |
|--------:|-----------|-------------|-----------------:|---------------------|
| 1 | **A1.1** | Absolute beginner | 50 | "Beginner — first contact" |
| 2 | **A1.2** | Beginner | 100 | "A1 Breakthrough" |
| 3 | **A2.1** | High beginner | 150 | "Elementary" |
| 4 | **A2.2** | Elementary | 200 | "A2 Waystage" |
| 5 | **B1.1** | Low intermediate | 300 | "Low Intermediate" |
| 6 | **B1.2** | Intermediate | 400 | "B1 Threshold" |
| 7 | **B2.1** | High intermediate | 500 | "Upper Intermediate" |
| 8 | **B2.2** | Upper intermediate | 600 | "B2 Vantage" |
| 9 | **C1** | Advanced | 800 | "C1 Effective Operational Proficiency" |
| 10 | **C2** | Mastery | 1,200 | "C2 Mastery" |

Note on A0: "A0" is not a CEFR level. The product uses **Section 1 (A1.1)** as the true zero start: the learner is assumed to know nothing, not even the Latin alphabet's English sound values.

## 2. Qualitative parameters at each level

The CEFR grades spoken and written performance on six parameters. These are the columns of every production rubric in doc 12.

| Parameter | A1 | A2 | B1 | B2 | C1 | C2 |
|-----------|----|----|----|----|----|----|
| **Range** | Isolated words, memorised chunks | Basic sentence patterns, memorised phrases; short groups | Enough to describe unpredictable situations with some circumlocution | Clear descriptions, viewpoints; no obvious searching for words | Broad repertoire; conveys finer shades; avoids difficulty smoothly | Fully exploits a comprehensive repertoire; reformulates precisely |
| **Accuracy** | Limited control of a few simple patterns | Some simple structures correctly; systematic basic mistakes | Reasonable accuracy in familiar contexts; L1 influence evident | Good control; occasional slips, non-systematic; can self-correct | Consistently high; errors rare and hard to spot | Consistent grammatical control of complex language, even under load |
| **Fluency** | Very short isolated utterances, much pausing | Short exchanges; false starts and reformulation very evident | Keeps going comprehensibly; pausing for grammar/lexis planning is evident | Fairly even tempo; few noticeably long pauses | Fluent, spontaneous, almost effortless; smooth flow | Natural, effortless; pauses only for rhetorical effect |
| **Interaction** | Asks/answers personal questions; depends on repetition & rephrasing | Simple routine exchange; can't sustain conversation | Can initiate, maintain, close simple face-to-face conversation | Initiates discourse, takes turn, ends conversation appropriately | Selects a suitable phrase to preface remarks; interweaves contribution | Interacts with ease and skill; picks up non-verbal and intonational cues |
| **Coherence** | Links words with *and*, *then* | Links groups with *and, but, because* | Links a series of shorter elements into a linear sequence | Clear connected discourse; limited "jumpiness" in a long contribution | Clear, smoothly flowing, well-structured; controlled organisational patterns | Coherent and cohesive with full and appropriate use of organisational patterns |
| **Phonology** | Pronunciation of a very limited repertoire needs much listener effort | Generally clear enough despite noticeable accent; interlocutors need to ask for repetition | Generally intelligible; L1 accent evident; occasional mispronunciation | Clear natural pronunciation and intonation | Varies intonation and places stress correctly to express finer shades | Conveys finer shades of meaning precisely through prosody |

**Intelligibility standard used by this course:** the target is *comfortable intelligibility for an international listener*, not accent elimination. A strong Uzbek-accented English that is fully intelligible and correctly stressed is a **success**, not a failure. Accent reduction beyond intelligibility is offered as an optional module, never as a requirement.

## 3. Level descriptors, can-do statements and exit gates

Each block below gives: the global descriptor; can-do statements per skill (these are the user-facing checklist); the linguistic load; and the exit gate (machine-checkable).

---

### 3.1 SECTION 1 — A1.1 (Absolute beginner)

**Global:** Can recognise and produce a small set of memorised words and formulaic phrases about self and immediate surroundings, in slow, clearly articulated speech with strong support.

**Can-do (user-facing):**
- Listening: Can recognise familiar words and very basic phrases (own name, numbers 0–100, days, family words) when people speak slowly and clearly.
- Reading: Can read the English alphabet; can decode familiar names, words and very simple sentences on signs, labels and posters.
- Spoken interaction: Can greet, say goodbye, say how they are, ask and answer *What's your name? / Where are you from? / How old are you?*
- Spoken production: Can say 3–5 sentences about themselves.
- Writing: Can write the alphabet, own name and address, numbers, and a 3-sentence self-introduction.
- Mediation: Can pass on a name, number or simple word to another person.

**Linguistic load:** 400 lexemes · 22 grammar points · full consonant inventory introduced · 8 vowel contrasts.

**Exit gate:**
- Alphabet & sound–letter mapping: ≥ 95% on the diagnostic.
- Numbers 0–100 recognition and production: ≥ 90%.
- 400 lexemes: ≥ 340 at SRS stability ≥ 7 days.
- Speaking: 30-second self-introduction, ≥ 70% intelligible words on ASR, ≥ 4 required content points.
- Node coverage: 96/96.

---

### 3.2 SECTION 2 — A1.2 (Beginner) → **CEFR A1 achieved**

**Global:** Can understand and use familiar everyday expressions and very basic phrases aimed at the satisfaction of needs of a concrete type. Can introduce themselves and others and can ask and answer questions about personal details. Can interact in a simple way provided the other person talks slowly and clearly and is prepared to help.

**Can-do:**
- Listening: Can follow speech that is very slow and carefully articulated, with long pauses; can understand simple directions, prices, times, and short classroom/shop instructions.
- Reading: Can understand short simple texts a single phrase at a time — menus, timetables, notices, a short personal message.
- Spoken interaction: Can ask for and give personal information; can buy something in a shop; can order food; can ask for and understand a price; can ask someone to repeat.
- Spoken production: Can describe where they live, their family, their daily routine, in a set of simple linked phrases.
- Writing: Can write a short simple postcard/message; can fill in a form with personal details; can write 60–80 words about themselves.
- Mediation: Can convey simple, predictable information from a notice or a short message to another person.

**Linguistic load:** cumulative 800 lexemes · cumulative 48 grammar points · present simple, present continuous (chunked), *be/have got*, articles, plurals, basic prepositions, imperatives, can (ability/permission), possessives.

**Exit gate:**
- 800 lexemes cumulative, ≥ 680 retained (stability ≥ 14 d).
- Level Exam A1 ≥ 75%, no section < 60%.
- Speaking: 60-second monologue + 6-turn scripted dialogue; rubric band A1 on all six parameters.
- Writing: 70-word personal message; ≥ 80% of sentences comprehensible; correct use of capitalisation and full stops.
- Listening: 8 short items at 90–110 wpm, ≥ 70%.
- Reading: 3 short texts of 40–60 words, ≥ 70%.

---

### 3.3 SECTION 3 — A2.1 (High beginner)

**Global:** Can handle short social exchanges and simple transactions; can describe past events in a simple way.

**Can-do:**
- Listening: Can understand phrases and vocabulary related to immediate personal relevance (family, shopping, local area, employment) at 110–130 wpm.
- Reading: Can find specific predictable information in simple everyday material — advertisements, prospectuses, menus, timetables. Can understand a short simple personal letter.
- Spoken interaction: Can handle very short social exchanges; can ask about and discuss what to do, where to go, and arrange to meet; can make and respond to invitations, suggestions and apologies.
- Spoken production: Can tell a simple story about the weekend; can describe a person, place or object in a series of simple phrases.
- Writing: Can write a series of simple phrases and sentences linked with *and, but, because*; can write a short simple note or message; 100–120 words.
- Mediation: Can relay in simple language the main point of a short spoken announcement or written notice.

**Linguistic load:** cumulative 1,300 lexemes · past simple regular + top 40 irregular verbs · countable/uncountable · comparatives · adverbs of frequency · *going to* · basic modals.

**Exit gate:** 1,300 lexemes (≥ 1,100 retained) · past-simple accuracy ≥ 80% in controlled production · 100-word narrative in the past · 5-turn transactional roleplay at ≥ 75% task completion · node coverage 112/112.

---

### 3.4 SECTION 4 — A2.2 (Elementary) → **CEFR A2 achieved**

**Global:** Can understand sentences and frequently used expressions related to areas of most immediate relevance. Can communicate in simple and routine tasks requiring a simple and direct exchange of information on familiar and routine matters. Can describe in simple terms aspects of their background, immediate environment and matters in areas of immediate need.

**Can-do (adds to A2.1):**
- Listening: Can catch the main point in short, clear, simple messages and announcements; can understand simple phone messages.
- Reading: Can understand short narratives and simple factual texts of 150–200 words; can identify main idea and specific details.
- Spoken interaction: Can deal with most situations likely to arise when travelling; can complain politely; can explain a problem; can ask for and follow directions.
- Spoken production: Can give a short, rehearsed 2-minute presentation on a familiar topic and answer simple follow-up questions.
- Writing: Can write a simple connected email of 120–150 words; can describe experiences and events in the past; can write a short review or a set of instructions.
- Mediation: Can summarise in simple language the content of a short, clearly structured text; can help two people understand each other in a routine transaction.

**Linguistic load:** cumulative 1,800 lexemes · present perfect (experience), past continuous, future forms, first conditional, comparative/superlative complete, quantifiers, adverb formation, infinitive vs -ing (first set), 8 core phrasal verbs.

**Exit gate:**
- 1,800 lexemes cumulative, ≥ 1,550 retained.
- Level Exam A2 ≥ 75%, no section < 60%.
- Writing: 150-word email, rubric band A2 across range/accuracy/coherence; ≥ 3 different linkers used correctly.
- Speaking: 2-minute rehearsed monologue + 8-turn unscripted roleplay; ≥ 60 words/minute; intelligibility ≥ 85%.
- Listening: 12 items at 120–140 wpm across 2 accents, ≥ 70%.
- Reading: 200-word texts, ≥ 75%.
- Immersion: ≥ 30,000 words read, ≥ 20 hours listened (logged).

---

### 3.5 SECTION 5 — B1.1 (Low intermediate)

**Global:** Can deal with most situations while travelling, produce connected text on familiar topics, and give brief reasons and explanations for opinions.

**Can-do:**
- Listening: Can understand the main points of clear standard speech on familiar matters at 140–150 wpm; can follow a short narrative or a simple news item.
- Reading: Can understand texts of 300–400 words consisting mainly of high-frequency everyday or job-related language; can understand the description of events, feelings and wishes in personal letters.
- Spoken interaction: Can enter unprepared into conversation on familiar topics; can express and respond to feelings; can agree and disagree politely; can ask for clarification and paraphrase.
- Spoken production: Can narrate a story or the plot of a book/film and describe reactions; can give brief reasons and explanations for opinions and plans.
- Writing: Can write straightforward connected text of 180–200 words on familiar topics; can write personal letters describing experiences and impressions.
- Mediation: Can relay the main content of longer texts; can take notes from a short talk; can explain a chart or a simple diagram in simple terms.

**Linguistic load:** cumulative 2,600 lexemes · present perfect vs past simple contrast fully · used to · second conditional · relative clauses (defining) · passive (present/past simple) · reported speech (statements) · modals of deduction (present) · verb patterns · 40 phrasal verbs.

**Exit gate:** 2,600 lexemes (≥ 2,200 retained) · present-perfect vs past-simple discrimination ≥ 80% · 200-word opinion text with ≥ 5 discourse markers · 3-minute unscripted monologue · Immersion ≥ 80,000 words read.

---

### 3.6 SECTION 6 — B1.2 (Intermediate) → **CEFR B1 achieved**

**Global:** Can understand the main points of clear standard input on familiar matters regularly encountered in work, school, leisure. Can deal with most situations likely to arise whilst travelling. Can produce simple connected text on topics which are familiar or of personal interest. Can describe experiences and events, dreams, hopes and ambitions and briefly give reasons and explanations for opinions and plans.

**Can-do (adds):**
- Listening: Can understand the main points of many radio/TV programmes on current affairs when delivery is relatively slow and clear; can follow a lecture on a familiar topic with visual support.
- Reading: Can read articles and reports of 500–600 words; can skim for gist and scan for detail; can understand a short story with a dictionary for < 5% of words.
- Spoken interaction: Can sustain a 10-minute conversation; can handle a job-interview-style exchange on a familiar role; can negotiate a simple problem (exchange, refund, complaint).
- Spoken production: Can give a prepared 5-minute talk with visual aids and handle questions.
- Writing: Can write 250-word essays, reports, reviews, formal emails; can summarise a 500-word text in 100 words.
- Mediation: Can summarise and comment on a text; can facilitate a simple group discussion; can translate the gist of an L1 text into simple English.

**Linguistic load:** cumulative 3,500 lexemes · full conditional set 0–3 (3rd introduced) · passive extended · reported speech complete · relative clauses non-defining · gerund/infinitive contrast systematised · modals of past deduction · *wish/if only* (present) · 80 phrasal verbs cumulative.

**Exit gate:**
- 3,500 lexemes cumulative, ≥ 3,000 retained.
- Level Exam B1 ≥ 75%, no section < 60%.
- Writing: 250-word opinion essay at B1 rubric band; paragraphing correct; ≥ 8 distinct linkers; error density ≤ 8 errors/100 words.
- Speaking: 5-minute prepared talk + 12-turn spontaneous discussion; ≥ 90 wpm; ≥ 4 repair strategies used.
- Listening: 20 items, 3 accents, 150 wpm, includes one 3-minute lecture extract; ≥ 70%.
- Reading: 600-word texts, 5 task types; ≥ 75%.
- Immersion: ≥ 150,000 words read, ≥ 60 hours listened, ≥ 10,000 words written, ≥ 15 hours spoken.

---

### 3.7 SECTION 7 — B2.1 (High intermediate)

**Global:** Can follow extended speech and complex lines of argument on reasonably familiar topics; can produce clear, detailed text and defend a viewpoint.

**Can-do:**
- Listening: Can understand extended speech and lectures at 160–180 wpm; can follow most TV news and current-affairs programmes; can understand most films in standard dialect.
- Reading: Can read articles and reports of 800–1,000 words concerned with contemporary problems in which writers adopt particular stances; can understand contemporary literary prose.
- Spoken interaction: Can take an active part in discussion in familiar contexts, accounting for and sustaining views; can interact with a degree of fluency and spontaneity that makes regular interaction with native speakers quite possible without strain for either party.
- Spoken production: Can present clear, detailed descriptions on a wide range of subjects; can explain a viewpoint giving advantages and disadvantages.
- Writing: Can write clear, detailed text of 300–350 words; essays and reports passing on information and giving reasons in support of or against a particular point of view.
- Mediation: Can summarise a wide range of factual and imaginative texts, commenting on and discussing contrasting points of view; can collaborate to construct meaning in a group.

**Linguistic load:** cumulative 4,700 lexemes · mixed conditionals · inversion after negative adverbials (receptive) · perfect modals · causative *have/get* · advanced passive (*it is said that…*) · participle clauses · complex noun phrases · hedging · 130 phrasal verbs.

**Exit gate:** 4,700 lexemes (≥ 4,000 retained) · 300-word argumentative essay at B2 band · error density ≤ 5/100 words · 6-minute discussion with turn-taking moves ≥ 8 · listening at 170 wpm ≥ 70% · Immersion ≥ 320,000 words read.

---

### 3.8 SECTION 8 — B2.2 (Upper intermediate) → **CEFR B2 achieved**

**Global:** Can understand the main ideas of complex text on both concrete and abstract topics, including technical discussions in their field of specialisation. Can interact with a degree of fluency and spontaneity that makes regular interaction with native speakers quite possible without strain for either party. Can produce clear, detailed text on a wide range of subjects and explain a viewpoint on a topical issue giving the advantages and disadvantages of various options.

**Can-do (adds):**
- Listening: Can understand recordings in non-standard accents and with background noise; can follow a fast-paced multi-speaker discussion and identify speaker stance.
- Reading: Can read with a large degree of independence, adapting style and speed to different texts; has a broad active reading vocabulary but may experience some difficulty with low-frequency idioms.
- Spoken interaction: Can adjust register; can argue a formal position; can handle disagreement without offence; can conduct and be interviewed.
- Spoken production: Can develop an argument systematically with appropriate highlighting of significant points and relevant supporting detail; can speak for 8 minutes without preparation.
- Writing: Can write reports, proposals, formal letters, reviews and discursive essays of 350–400 words with clear structure, appropriate register and coherent argumentation.
- Mediation: Can act as an informal interpreter in predictable situations; can synthesise information from several sources into a coherent summary.

**Exit gate:**
- 6,000 lexemes cumulative, ≥ 5,000 retained; ≥ 1,200 collocations; ≥ 150 phrasal verbs.
- Level Exam B2 ≥ 75%, no section < 60%.
- Writing: two tasks (essay 350 w + transactional 250 w) at B2 band; register appropriate; cohesion score ≥ 0.75.
- Speaking: 8-minute unprepared discourse + 15-turn interaction; ≥ 120 wpm; self-repair used, other-repair rarely needed.
- Listening: 25 items at 170–185 wpm, 5 accents, one with background noise; ≥ 70%.
- Reading: 1,000-word texts, timed at ≥ 150 wpm reading speed with ≥ 75% comprehension.
- Immersion: ≥ 500,000 words read, ≥ 150 h listened, ≥ 30,000 words written, ≥ 40 h spoken.

---

### 3.9 SECTION 9 — C1 (Advanced) → **CEFR C1 achieved**

**Global:** Can understand a wide range of demanding, longer texts, and recognise implicit meaning. Can express themselves fluently and spontaneously without much obvious searching for expressions. Can use language flexibly and effectively for social, academic and professional purposes. Can produce clear, well-structured, detailed text on complex subjects, showing controlled use of organisational patterns, connectors and cohesive devices.

**Can-do:**
- Listening: Can understand extended speech even when it is not clearly structured and when relationships are only implied; can understand television programmes and films without too much effort; can follow rapid overlapping speech at 190–210 wpm; can detect irony, understatement and speaker attitude.
- Reading: Can understand long and complex factual and literary texts, appreciating distinctions of style; can understand specialised articles and longer technical instructions even outside their field; reading speed ≥ 200 wpm with ≥ 80% comprehension.
- Spoken interaction: Can express themselves fluently and spontaneously, almost effortlessly; can use language flexibly for social and professional purposes; can formulate ideas and opinions with precision and relate their contribution skilfully to those of other speakers.
- Spoken production: Can present clear, detailed descriptions of complex subjects integrating sub-themes, developing particular points and rounding off with an appropriate conclusion; can hold the floor for 15 minutes.
- Writing: Can express themselves in clear, well-structured text, expanding and supporting points of view at some length with subsidiary points, reasons and relevant examples; can select style appropriate to the reader; 500 words.
- Mediation: Can act as an effective mediator between speakers of the target language and L1 speakers; can convey clearly the salient points and nuance of complex texts; can manage group dynamics and encourage participation.

**Linguistic load:** cumulative 9,000 lexemes · full inversion productively · cleft sentences · fronting · nominalisation · subjunctive/formal *were* · discourse-level ellipsis and substitution · advanced hedging & boosting · idiom (≥ 300) · 200+ phrasal verbs · academic word list complete.

**Exit gate:**
- 9,000 lexemes cumulative, ≥ 7,500 retained; ≥ 2,500 collocations; ≥ 300 idioms.
- Level Exam C1 ≥ 75%, no section < 65%.
- Writing: 500-word essay + 300-word register-shifted task; C1 band; lexical sophistication (proportion of items beyond the 3,000 band) ≥ 20%; error density ≤ 2.5/100 words.
- Speaking: 15-minute seminar-style interaction, chaired turn; ≥ 140 wpm; pausing ratio < 12%; hedging and stance markers ≥ 10 distinct.
- Listening: 30 items at 190–210 wpm including implicit-meaning items; ≥ 72%.
- Reading: 1,500–2,000-word texts at ≥ 200 wpm with ≥ 80% comprehension; inference items ≥ 70%.
- Immersion: ≥ 1.2 M words read (incl. ≥ 6 full books), ≥ 320 h listened, ≥ 70,000 words written, ≥ 80 h spoken.

---

### 3.10 SECTION 10 — C2 (Mastery) → **CEFR C2 achieved**

**Global:** Can understand with ease virtually everything heard or read. Can summarise information from different spoken and written sources, reconstructing arguments and accounts in a coherent presentation. Can express themselves spontaneously, very fluently and precisely, differentiating finer shades of meaning even in more complex situations.

C2 is **not** "native-like." It is *mastery of the language as a tool*: precision, register control, and the ability to reconstruct and reformulate.

**Can-do:**
- Listening: Can understand any kind of spoken language, live or broadcast, at native speed, including rapid, idiomatic, regionally-accented or acoustically degraded speech, given some time to get familiar with the accent.
- Reading: Can read with ease virtually all forms of the written language, including abstract, structurally or linguistically complex texts such as manuals, specialised articles and literary works; appreciates stylistic nuance, allusion and irony.
- Spoken interaction: Can take part effortlessly in any conversation or discussion with good familiarity with idiomatic expressions and colloquialisms; can express themselves fluently and convey finer shades of meaning precisely; can backtrack and restructure around a difficulty so smoothly the interlocutor is hardly aware of it.
- Spoken production: Can present a clear, smoothly-flowing description or argument in a style appropriate to the context and with an effective logical structure which helps the recipient to notice and remember significant points; can improvise a 20-minute talk.
- Writing: Can write clear, smoothly-flowing text in an appropriate style; can write complex letters, reports or articles which present a case with an effective logical structure; can write summaries and reviews of professional or literary works; 700+ words, any genre.
- Mediation: Can interpret between languages, mediate cultural difference, reconstruct arguments from multiple conflicting sources, and reformulate complex specialised material for a lay audience without loss of nuance.

**Linguistic load:** cumulative 12,000 productive / 20,000+ receptive lexemes · all structures productive · stylistic variation (archaic, literary, legal, journalistic, technical) · phonological control of stress-shift, tone-unit segmentation and emphatic prosody · full idiom, proverb, cultural allusion and register humour.

**Exit gate:**
- 12,000 lexemes productive, 20,000+ receptive (tested by a Vocabulary Size Test sample across 20 frequency bands).
- Level Exam C2 ≥ 80%, no section < 70%.
- Writing: three tasks in three genres and registers (academic argument 700 w; professional proposal 500 w; creative/review 500 w); C2 band on all; register discrimination judged blind by two raters.
- Speaking: 20-minute unprepared presentation + adversarial Q&A; ≥ 160 wpm; ≤ 1 unresolved communication breakdown; measurable prosodic marking of contrast, concession and irony.
- Listening: 35 items, native-speed authentic media at 200–230 wpm, 8 accents, incl. degraded audio; ≥ 75%.
- Reading: 3,000-word specialised and literary texts; inference, tone and stylistic items ≥ 75%.
- Mediation task: reconstruct a coherent 400-word account from four conflicting sources; scored on completeness, accuracy and neutrality.
- Immersion: ≥ 2.5 M words read (incl. ≥ 15 full books, ≥ 3 pre-1950), ≥ 600 h listened, ≥ 150,000 words written, ≥ 160 h spoken.

## 4. Typical interlanguage profile per level (what errors are *expected*)

Knowing which errors are developmentally normal prevents the app from over-correcting and demotivating. Feedback intensity is tuned to this table.

| Level | Errors that are normal and should be lightly flagged | Errors that must be actively corrected |
|-------|------------------------------------------------------|----------------------------------------|
| A1 | Missing articles, 3rd-person -s omission, word order in questions | Sound–letter decoding, *be* omission, pronoun gender |
| A2 | Past irregular over-regularisation (*goed*), preposition choice, article system | Tense choice past vs present, question formation, plural -s |
| B1 | Present perfect vs past simple, article with abstract nouns, phrasal-verb separability | Verb pattern (*enjoy to do*), conditional forms, reported-speech backshift |
| B2 | Collocation slips, subtle article use, hedging under-use | Register mixing, run-on sentences, overuse of simple linkers, L1 calques |
| C1 | Idiom near-misses, rare-preposition choice, rhythm/stress in long NPs | Nominalisation misuse, cohesion breakdown in long text, overformality |
| C2 | Stylistic infelicity, connotation shading | Anything that impedes precision or register control |

**Fossilisation watchlist** (items that stop improving without explicit intervention — the scheduler flags any item stuck at low stability across 6+ reviews and escalates it to a Focus Clinic, see doc 07 §7):
articles · 3rd-person -s · present perfect · prepositions of time/place · countable/uncountable · word stress · /θ ð/ · /v w/ · /ɪ iː/ · adjective/adverb confusion · *make/do* · word order of adverbs.
